Candied Yams

a little rum never hurt anybody

Sweet potatoes parboiled in vinegar water so they hold their shape, then glazed in one skillet with brown sugar, orange, fresh ginger and dark rum or bourbon. A raw splash of the spirit and fresh zest go on at the very end, where you can still smell them.

Serves6–8 (full) · 3–4 (half) Activeabout 25 min Bake20–35 min at 375°F / 190°C Make aheadup to 2 days

Equipment: one oven-safe stainless skillet (12-inch for the full batch, 10-inch for the half), a colander and a microplane.

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 375°F / 190°C. Rub the first measure of zest into the granulated sugar with your fingertips for a minute or two, until it looks like damp orange sand. Why: the sugar crystals break open the oil pockets in the zest and soak up the oil, with no heat to drive off the brightest notes.
  2. Parboil in vinegar water. Bring the water and vinegar to a boil in the skillet. Add the cubes and simmer 6–8 min, until a knife meets slight resistance at the center. Drain. Why: acid slows how fast the outside of each cube softens, so the cubes hold their shape while the centers cook through. It prevents the classic failure: glazed outside, firm inside.
  3. Start the syrup. Return the empty skillet to medium-low for a few seconds to dry it, then melt the butter. Take the pan off the burner, pour in the first measure of rum or bourbon, return it to the heat and simmer 1 min. Why: pouring off the burner keeps the alcohol from flaring.
  4. Build the syrup. Add the orange sugar, brown sugar, orange juice, peel, lemon juice, water, salt, cinnamon, ginger and nutmeg. Simmer 3–4 min, until glossy and the bubbles turn large and slow.
  5. Coat the cubes. Add the sweet potatoes and simmer 2–3 min, spooning the syrup over them. Remove the peel.
  6. Bake uncovered, basting every 10 min: 25–35 min for the full batch, 20–25 min for the half. They're done when a knife slides through the center and the syrup coats the back of a spoon. If the syrup is still thin, reduce it on the stovetop for 2–3 min. Why: the open pan lets the syrup thicken into a glaze; basting keeps the tops from drying out.
  7. Finish off the heat. Stir in the raw rum or bourbon, spoon the syrup over, and scatter the fresh zest and a pinch of flaky salt. Why: heat destroys fresh orange aroma and the spirit's vanilla and caramel notes. Added at the end, both reach the table.

Notes

Make ahead

Cook through step 6, cool, cover and refrigerate up to 2 days.

Reheat loosely covered with foil at 375–425°F for about 20 min, until hot through. The foil keeps the sugar at the edges from scorching.

Do step 7 only after reheating.

Rum or bourbon

Dark or aged rum leans into the brown sugar and molasses. Bourbon brings oak and vanilla. Spiced rum works; skip white rum.

The finishing splash stays alcoholic. Leave it out, or use a little extra orange juice, if anyone avoids alcohol.

Cleanup

Hardened sugar dissolves in water. Fill the skillet, simmer 5 minutes, and pour it out. No scraping.

Avoid bare cast iron; the lemon juice strips its seasoning.
