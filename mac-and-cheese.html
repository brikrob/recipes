Grown-Up Mac & Cheese

no blue box was harmed

A baked mac with herb-infused cream, roasted garlic, miso, sharp cheddar and Parmigiano, a stretchy mozzarella middle and a crisp za’atar crumb. The pasta goes in half-cooked and finishes in the oven by soaking up the sauce, which is why the sauce looks loose at first.

Equipment: a 2.5-qt (11×7-inch) baking dish for the full batch or a shallow 1.5-qt gratin dish for the half, a saucepan, a small skillet, a fine strainer and a thermometer.

Method

  1. Roast the garlic. Halve a head crosswise, drizzle with oil, wrap in foil and roast at 375–400°F until soft and golden, about 45 min. Squeeze out what you need.
  2. Infuse the dairy. Heat the milk, cream, bay, thyme, rosemary, garlic and bouillon until steaming, about 80°C / 175°F, without boiling. Cover off the heat for 20 min, then strain. Why: The herb flavor dissolves into the milk fat. Boiling scorches the milk sugars and forms a skin.
  3. Make the topping. Brown the butter in a small skillet until the solids are hazelnut-colored and smell nutty. Toast the panko in it until golden, then stir in the za’atar off the heat. Why: Pre-toasting browns the crumbs evenly. Za’atar’s sesame and sumac scorch in a hot pan.
  4. Par-boil the pasta for 5 minutes only. Drain and rinse briefly with cold water without tossing. Why: The pasta finishes in the oven by absorbing sauce, which is what lets this much sauce set.
  5. Make the roux. Melt the butter over medium, whisk in the flour and cook 1–2 min, staying blond. Add the mustard, pepper, cayenne and nutmeg for the last 30 seconds.
  6. Whisk in the warm dairy, a third first, then the rest. Simmer, whisking, until the sauce coats a spoon, 3–5 min. Why: Flour only thickens fully once it simmers. Expect a sauce lighter than a standard white sauce.
  7. Off the heat, whisk in the sodium citrate (if using) until dissolved, then the roasted garlic, then the miso thinned with a ladle of sauce. Let the pot sit 2–3 min until it’s below about 75°C / 165°F.
  8. Add the cheddar and Parmigiano a handful at a time, each fully melted before the next. Why: Past about 75°C the cheese proteins tighten and squeeze out fat, and the sauce turns grainy. Sodium citrate widens that margin; without it, temperature is your only protection.
  9. Taste, then salt. The cheese, miso and bouillon already bring plenty.
  10. Fold in the pasta. It should look loose, almost soupy. That liquid is what the pasta drinks in the oven.
  11. Layer. In a buttered dish set on a sheet pan: half the mac, all the mozzarella, then the rest. Scatter the topping evenly. Why: In the middle, the mozzarella gives you the cheese pull without blocking the crumbs from crisping.
  12. Bake at 400°F / 200°C until the edges bubble and the center reaches 165°F / 74°C: 25–35 min for the full batch, 20–25 min for the half. Tent with foil if the top browns early.
  13. Rest 10 minutes, then scatter the green onion. Why: The sauce thickens as it cools slightly, so it’s creamy rather than runny.

Notes

Sodium citrate: optional

It’s an emulsifying salt, the thing that makes processed cheese melt smoothly. A little keeps the cheddar and Parmigiano from turning grainy, and the leftovers reheat creamier.

With it: use the smaller flour amount, 26 g (13 g for a half batch).

Without it: use the full 30 g (15 g) of flour, keep the sauce below 75°C when the cheese goes in, and reheat gently.

Make ahead

The sauce (through step 9) and the topping keep 1 day. Press plastic onto the sauce and refrigerate; keep the topping airtight.

Rewarm the sauce gently with a splash of milk until steaming, not boiling.

Don’t assemble ahead. Par-boiled pasta left in the sauce overnight absorbs all of it.

If the sauce breaks

Grainy or oily? Off the heat, whisk in a splash of cold milk, a little at a time, until it comes back together.

Shred your own cheese. Pre-shredded is coated in anti-caking starch and melts gritty.

Cleanup: soak the pans in warm, not hot, water. Very hot water cooks the cheese protein on harder.
